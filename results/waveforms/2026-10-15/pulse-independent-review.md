The frequency definitions agree with [ETSI EN302307-1 V1.4.1,5.6/AnnexA](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/302300_302399/30230701/01.04.01_60/en_30230701v010401p.pdf): normalized frequency is f/(Rs/2), digital omega is pi*f/N, and DAC hold amplitude is sinc(f/(2N)). AnnexA calls for rectangular-input correction; checking a compensated digital FIR without its DAC hold is a different model. Symmetric even-length FIRs have digital delay(L*N−1)/(2N) symbols and delayL/2 after hold. The implementation removes this constant delay, not a varying phase term. In the constrained passband the centered real response is positive, so the group-delay mask has no hidden phase discontinuity.

The LP fixes the ideal-receiver main sample to1 to remove trivial scaling. The spectral mask is normalized to positive DC. After scaling/quantization, both main and DC change together; normalized ISI and frequency response remain scale independent. This does not assert identical average RF power among modulation modes: fixed peak headroom, average signal power, and receiver gain are different quantities, and the audit records actual sample RMS/main gain.

An independent infinite-symbol check avoids the solver's finiteK ISI tail. For centered cascade responseP(f)=FIR(f)*hold(f)*idealRRC(f), supported on|f|≤1.2, the symbol-sampled alias response is A(f)=P(f)+P(2−f) on0.8≤f≤1, and P(f) below0.8. Infinite ISI is the RMS of A/main−1 on0..1 by Parseval. Adaptive quadrature agrees with direct midpoint integration of the exact finite held pulse into the analytic continuous RRC at increasing oversampling. This catches normalization, sample-center, ZOH, and finite-receiver-span mistakes. The old no-hold0.97% L9 result is not the correct comparison for a hold-co-designed pulse; its matching held result is1.531%.

Frequency verification explicitly includes every AnnexA knot. More than1,048,000 direct full-tap evaluations are supplemented by a global derivative bound for both response and piecewise dB envelope, multiplied by half the largest frequency gap. Positive remaining linear-amplitude slack certifies all points between grid samples within the stated window. Exact knot evaluation slightly revises some old FFT-grid margins without changing any pass/fail result.

Final LUT rounding is nonlinear. For each binary phase LUT, an exact integer Walsh transform expands the output into products of independent±1 symbols. Grouping products that differ only by a time translation produces mutually uncorrelated white driving processes, each with a finite pulse. The **zero-mean iid ensemble** PSD is exactly the sum of their pulse power spectra; no white-quantization-noise assumption is used. The receiver audit includes every branch, its own symbol aliases, unequal A/B main gains, and deterministic DC bias. Two-plane8PSK uses the actual rounded Q30-weighted A/B coefficient and table arithmetic. Uniform8PSK gives independent A/B signs on either individual axis; I/Q correlations do not change summed per-axis error power.

The zero-mean continuous PSD excludes deterministic phase-dependent LUT mean spectral lines. They are reported separately as `periodic_mean_lsb_per_phase`, mean power, and continuous DC bias; receiver EVM includes DC bias. These means are tiny fractions of one DAC LSB, but excluding them is an explicit scope condition. The audit does not assert that deterministic PLHEADER/pilot/coded streams are iid or establish an RF spectral measurement.

The model checks N4 over0..6 f/(Rs/2), and N2 over0..2 only. First sampling-image centers are8 and4 respectively; images, actual AFE magnitude/group delay, DAC mismatch/jitter, PA distortion, IQ skew, and receiver decode are unqualified. Memory arithmetic—64KiB QPSKquad,64KiB two-plane8PSK,72KiB exact16APSK chunk tables—is valid for the stated spans, but is not a PIO address/timing or whole-pipeline proof. The LP supporting-cut gap is a finite-model convex optimization result, not a global RF optimum.
